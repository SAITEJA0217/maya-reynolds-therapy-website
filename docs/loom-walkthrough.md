# Loom walkthrough — Dr. Maya Reynolds (≈5 minutes)

*Audience: Dr. Reynolds. Tone: showing a client the first draft of her site. Avoid technical terms.*

Before recording: open the site at full desktop width, scrolled to the top. Keep a phone-sized window, or your browser's device view at 390px, ready for the final section.

---

### 0:00–0:30 · Opening

"Hi Maya, thanks for making the time. This is the first draft of your new homepage. I'll take you through it the way a new client would see it, explain why it looks the way it does, and show you how it works on a phone. At the end, let me know what feels right and what doesn't."

### 0:30–1:15 · First impression (hero)

- "Within a few seconds visitors can tell where you are, who you help and how to reach you. The line at the top says in-person in Santa Monica and telehealth across California."
- "The headline says plainly what you do and where: therapy in Santa Monica for anxiety, trauma and burnout, in a calm place to work through them. That helps the right people, and search engines, recognise you straight away, and we deliberately avoided the usual 'your healing journey starts here' wording."
- "That's your actual office in the main photo, not a stock image. The colours on the whole site come from that room: the linen walls, the olive tree, the brick."
- Point to **Request an appointment** and **Meet Dr. Reynolds**: "There's one clear next step, plus a softer option for people who want to learn about you first."

### 1:15–2:00 · Recognition (intro, who I help, quote)

- Scroll to "You look fine from the outside…": "This part is meant to make a visitor feel understood. The details (constant worry, trouble sleeping, always bracing) come straight from your own words."
- "Who I help" shows three kinds of client from your profile: high-achievers running on empty, adults affected by earlier experiences, and entrepreneurs, creatives and professionals. "Each one is paired with a corner of your office."
- Dark band: "This quiet pause is your line about therapy being a place to slow down and reconnect."

### 2:00–2:50 · Trust (areas of focus, about, specialties)

- Areas of focus: "A quick list so visitors can check that you work with what they're dealing with."
- About: "Here's your portrait, your role and your approach: warm, collaborative, grounded. I've listed the four methods you named (CBT, EMDR, mindfulness and body-oriented work) in plain language with no jargon."
- Specialties: "You asked for exactly three focus areas: anxiety and panic, trauma and EMDR, and burnout and perfectionism. Each description only uses what's in your profile, for example that trauma work is carefully paced with an emphasis on safety and stabilization. I haven't added any claims, credentials or promises you didn't make."

### 2:50–3:40 · The office (new section)

- "This section is new. The site we modelled the layout on doesn't have one, but your profile mentions that clients say the room itself helps them relax, so it deserved its own place."
- Show the spread: "The large photo is the therapy room itself; the smaller one shows the light through those tall windows. Earlier on the page you only see details; here is the whole room. The line in italics is from your profile: clients say the space itself helps them feel at ease. The goal is for someone to picture themselves arriving before they've even booked."
- "Underneath are the practical details: your address with a link to directions, telehealth for clients located in California, and what the space is like."

### 3:40–4:10 · Questions and getting in touch

- Open one FAQ: "These answer the questions people ask before they reach out: who you work with, what sessions feel like, in-person or telehealth, and whether you'd be a good fit. Every answer is based on your profile."
- Contact: "The request form is short: name and email, an optional phone number, how they'd like to meet, and an optional note about what's bringing them in. It checks entries as people fill it in, gently reminds them not to share private clinical details, and makes clear it isn't for emergencies, with 988 and 911 right there."
- Footer: "Your address and specialties again, plus a 988 crisis line, which is good practice for any therapy site."

### 4:10–4:45 · Mobile

Switch to the phone view.

- "Most people will find you on their phone, so everything is built for that. The menu opens full-screen with large, easy-to-tap links."
- Scroll quickly: "Sections stack cleanly, the text stays easy to read, and there's always a way to get in touch."

### 4:45–5:00 · Close and your input

"To go live I need three things from you: a phone number or email you want public, confirmation that you're happy for the office photos and portrait to be used, and where you'd like appointment requests to go. Anything you'd like changed, just tell me. Thanks, Maya."

**Reminders while recording:** scroll slowly, let each section settle for a beat, and keep the cursor still while you talk.
